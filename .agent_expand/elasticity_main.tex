\subsection{Diagnosing the Active Frontier}

The three-term law can diagnose the active bottleneck locally.  Write
\begin{equation}
L(m,T,N)=C_m m^{-\delta}+C_T(\eta T)^{-\gamma}+C_NN^{-\beta},
\label{eq:elasticity-proxy}
\end{equation}
where $\delta=b-1$, $\gamma=(b-1)/a$, and $\beta=(b-1)/s$.
Define the positive logarithmic elasticities
\[
\begin{aligned}
e_m&=-\partial_{\log m}\log L, &
e_T&=-\partial_{\log T}\log L, &
e_N&=-\partial_{\log N}\log L.
\end{aligned}
\]

\begin{proposition}[Frontier weights]
\label{prop:elasticity}
For Eq.~\eqref{eq:elasticity-proxy},
\begin{equation}
\frac{e_m}{\delta}+\frac{e_T}{\gamma}+\frac{e_N}{\beta}=1.
\label{eq:elasticity-simplex}
\end{equation}
Each normalized elasticity equals the fraction of predicted risk contributed by
its corresponding term.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Differentiate Eq.~\eqref{eq:elasticity-proxy} with respect to the three
logarithmic resources and divide by $L$.
\end{proof}

Thus the normalized elasticities form a scale-local phase map without requiring
the constants $C_m,C_T,C_N$.  A large width, update, or unique-data weight
identifies a capacity, optimization, or coverage bottleneck, respectively.  The
optimization--coverage boundary satisfies
$C_T(\eta T)^{-\gamma}\asymp C_NN^{-\beta}$, recovering
$K=T/N\asymp\eta^{-1}N^{a/s-1}$.  The measured slopes probe all three corners:
$-0.991$ versus $-\delta=-1$ for width, $-0.459$ versus
$-\gamma=-1/2$ for early updates, and $-0.655$ versus
$-\beta=-2/3$ for unique data.  Finite differences on neighboring logarithmic
scale points therefore provide a practical rule: increase the resource with the
largest normalized elasticity, and stop replay when its weight becomes small.
