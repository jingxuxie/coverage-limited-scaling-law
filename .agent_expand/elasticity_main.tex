\paragraph{Local frontier diagnostic.}
Let $L=L_m+L_T+L_N$ denote the three terms in the additive law, with exponents
$\delta=b-1$, $\gamma=(b-1)/a$, and $\beta=(b-1)/s$. Their risk shares
\[
\omega_m=\frac{L_m}{L},\qquad
\omega_T=\frac{L_T}{L},\qquad
\omega_N=\frac{L_N}{L}
\]
satisfy $\omega_m+\omega_T+\omega_N=1$. Equivalently, they are the normalized
logarithmic elasticities $-\partial_{\log m}\log L/\delta$,
$-\partial_{\log T}\log L/\gamma$, and
$-\partial_{\log N}\log L/\beta$ (proof in the supplement). The largest
weight identifies the active bottleneck; $\omega_T\asymp\omega_N$ recovers
$K\asymp\eta^{-1}N^{a/s-1}$. Measured slopes probe all three corners:
$-0.991$ versus $-1$ for width, $-0.459$ versus $-1/2$ for updates, and
$-0.655$ versus $-2/3$ for unique data. Local finite differences therefore
indicate which resource to expand next.
